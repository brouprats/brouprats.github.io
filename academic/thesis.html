Question

Recurrent neural networks are built to capture non-linear dynamics and long memory. Do they beat linear models on market indices, and do macroeconomic variables help either of them?

4indices on three continents
30years of monthly data
8models per index
6error metrics

Method

Data

S&P 500, Nasdaq Composite, Euro Stoxx 50 and Nikkei 225, 1994—2024, from the St. Louis Fed, yfinance and the ECB. Strict chronological split: training on 1994—2017, testing on monthly forecasts from January 2018 to December 2023.

Exogenous variables

Inflation, unemployment, interest rate and the political cycle (which of the two main blocs led each region).

Statistical models

ARIMA(1,1,1) for every index, chosen from ADF stationarity tests and ACF/PACF, validated with Ljung-Box tests on the residuals. ARIMAX adds the exogenous variables.

Neural networks

Six LSTM configurations: univariate or multivariate, with look-back windows of 1, 3 and 6 months. 32 units, dropout 0.1, Adam at 10⁻³, 30 epochs, fixed seed.

ARIMA wins everywhere

On all four indices and all six metrics. On the S&P 500 the best LSTM's error is two and a half times ARIMA's.

Macro data does not help

ARIMAX never improves on ARIMA, and adding the same variables to the LSTM often makes it worse.

Look-back matters for LSTMs

A one-month window is unusable; six months cuts the error by a factor of six on the S&P 500.

Why

Monthly averages smooth volatility and leave about 300 training points, too few for a network to show its strength. The result is consistent with efficient markets: public monthly macro data is already in the price.

Limits and next steps

One ARIMA order for every index, monthly data and no significance testing of the exogenous variables. The natural extensions are daily data, GRU networks, systematic hyperparameter search and a cointegration (VECM) treatment where the covariates are not stationary. The full thesis is written in Catalan and available on request.
